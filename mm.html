The objective is mean P&L minus ½κ times its variance, and the highlighted row scores highest on it. Mean P&L shows ± one standard error over paths. The best tuned fixed rule is chosen from half-spreads 0.30–1.80 on the same objective, so it is a strong baseline. A–S has no structural advantage on mean P&L. Its edge is that it skews quotes to shed inventory, which shows up as lower variance and a smaller terminal position.

What to watch. When A–S gets long, its reservation price (blue) drops below mid, so the ask moves closer and sell fills become more likely. Inventory gets pulled back toward zero. The fixed-spread quoter, on the same path, lets inventory wander with the order flow.

What the policy does in each state: reservation-price offset r − S

|skew| = half-spread

Blue means quotes are shifted up (short inventory, so it wants to buy) and red means shifted down (long inventory). Outside the dashed amber lines the skew is bigger than the half-spread, so one side quotes through the mid to unload. The band widens as the session ends, because the risk term γσ²τ shrinks.
